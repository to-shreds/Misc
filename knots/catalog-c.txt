# Knotbook original companion instructions. Publisher reference: header slug.

@highwaymans-hitch|Highwayman's Hitch|highwaymans-hitch-knot|Quick release|2|Camping|Bank robber's knot
=A historical quick-release hitch to study with an unloaded practice line.
+Soft cord and a horizontal practice pole.
!Can collapse or release unexpectedly. Not for climbing, animal restraint, or an unattended load; compare the Tumble Hitch instead.
?The final release bight holds the earlier bights, and pulling the free tail withdraws it.
xConfusing which bight receives the load changes the hitch's behaviour.
>Hold the first bight::Make a bight on the standing side and hold it against the front of the pole.
>Wrap a second bight::Take a doubled section of the remaining rope behind the pole and around the first bight, following the demonstrated crossing.
>Make the release fold::Fold the free tail into a third bight, leaving a visible release end.
>Lock the bights::Pass that tail bight through the first bight on the front of the pole.
>Inspect unloaded::Snug the interlocking folds with your hands. Practice releasing it without any load by pulling the free tail.

@common-whipping|Common Whipping|common-whipping-knot|Rope care|2|Everyday,Boating|Plain whipping
=Bind a rope end with twine to help stop its strands fraying.
+Whipping twine, a practice rope and scissors used with care.
!A whipping is a rope-end finish, not a load-bearing termination or a repair for damaged rope.
?Close wraps cover the rope, with the twine ends buried and no loose turn at either edge.
xLeaving gaps between wraps allows the binding to loosen and the strands to spread.
>Lay the twine along the rope::Hold a short twine tail against the rope near its end, leaving plenty of long end for the wraps.
>Build the first wraps::Wind the long end snugly around the rope and the short tail for about eight adjacent turns.
>Fold the short tail::Turn the remaining short end back into a narrow bight lying along the rope.
>Cover the bight::Continue with about eight more wraps over the bight, then pass the working twine end through its exposed tip.
>Bury the join::Pull the short tail so the working end disappears beneath the wraps. Check the binding before carefully trimming the twine ends.

@hunters-bend|Hunter's Bend|hunters-bend-knot|Bend|3|Camping,Decorative|Rigger's bend
=Join similar ropes with two interlocked overhand structures.
+Two similar soft cords in contrasting colours.
!Can jam tightly after loading. Several similar-looking bends have different crossings; do not identify this one by silhouette alone.
?The two overhand structures interlock, with both tails following the demonstrated central route.
xChanging the tails' paths through the middle makes a different bend.
>Make two bights::Fold each rope end back into a U, leaving long tails.
>Interlock the folds::Place the two bights through one another so their curved ends overlap.
>Cross each tail::Take each working end across its own standing part and over the other rope.
>Thread through the middle::Guide each end across the shared centre, beneath both ropes, then above its own returning strand. Follow one colour at a time.
>Dress the join::Take up slack a little at a time from all four ends. Compare every crossing with the demonstration before tightening.

@icicle-hitch|Icicle Hitch|icicle-hitch-knot-end-method|Friction hitch|3|Boating,Specialist|
=A directional gripping hitch tied around a smooth pole.
+Soft cord and a smooth horizontal dowel.
!Designed for pull parallel to the pole in one direction. Performance depends on the cord and surface; not personal-support instruction.
?Four close wraps sit away from the pull, with the locking section beside the standing end.
xPulling sideways away from the pole does not test the intended gripping direction.
>Choose the direction::Identify which way along the pole the standing part will pull.
>Make four wraps::Wind the working end around the pole four times, progressing away from that direction.
>Leave a hanging loop::Keep a loop hanging beneath the pole while taking the tail behind the standing part and over the pole.
>Complete the locking turns::Continue behind the standing part once more, over the pole again, then bring the tail down beside the standing part.
>Dress the grip::Snug the turns neatly and compare the locking path. Test only with a light hand pull parallel to the dowel.

@improved-clinch|Improved Clinch Knot|improved-clinch-knot|Fishing attachment|1|Fishing|
=Attach a fishing line to a hook, swivel or lure eye.
+Practice monofilament and a blunt ring.
!Not a universal choice for slippery braid or heavy line. Follow the line manufacturer's knot guidance and cover sharp hooks.
?The tag passes through the small eye-side loop and then through the larger loop made by that first tuck.
xStopping after the first tuck makes the ordinary clinch, not the improved version.
>Thread the eye::Pass the tag through the eye and leave enough line for several wraps.
>Make the coils::Wrap the tag around the standing line five or six times, keeping the coils adjacent.
>Use the small loop::Bring the tag back and pass it through the opening immediately beside the eye.
>Add the improvement::Feed the tag through the new, larger loop created by the previous tuck.
>Set slowly::Moisten as appropriate and draw the knot down evenly, helping the coils settle before testing and trimming.

@klemheist|Klemheist Knot|klemheist-knot|Friction hitch|2|Specialist|Machard knot
=A directional friction hitch made with a closed cord loop.
+A suitably joined practice cord loop and a thicker host rope.
!Rope compatibility and complete system configuration need qualified instruction. Never experiment with a person suspended from this practice hitch.
?The long loop passes through the short loop and folds back in the gripping direction.
xLoading it in the reverse direction, or using an unsuitable slick cord, can prevent gripping.
>Lay the cord loop beside the rope::Arrange a short loop end and a longer loop end, with the joining knot clear of the future wraps.
>Wrap the host rope::Use the long loop to make several neat turns around the host rope, working along it.
>Keep one short eye exposed::Leave the short end visible below the coil stack.
>Pass long through short::Feed the long loop through the short eye, then draw it back down beside the wraps.
>Dress for inspection::Remove slack and align all turns. Use only a light hand pull in the intended direction to observe how it grips.

@lanyard|Lanyard Knot|lanyard-knot|Decorative|3|Decorative|Diamond knot,Friendship knot
=Make a rounded decorative button below a cord loop.
+One long soft cord; learn the flat Carrick Bend first.
!Decoration only. Do not make non-breakaway neckwear or assume the button is a rated attachment.
?The flat Carrick centre becomes a round button; both tails emerge together opposite the loop.
xSkipping the final outside-and-up tucks leaves a flat Carrick structure rather than a lanyard button.
>Preserve the top loop::Fold the cord, keeping its middle bight as the future lanyard eye.
>Lay out a Carrick centre::Use the two tails to make a loose, flat Carrick Bend beneath that eye. Follow the Carrick tutorial for its alternating crossings.
>Bring the first tail around::Take one tail around the outside of the eye's roots, then up through the centre of the flat knot.
>Repeat with the other tail::Bring the second tail around its side and up through the same centre, beside the first.
>Shape the button::Ease slack around each turn in sequence. Tighten gradually until the flat layout becomes a round button with a fixed eye above it.

@lightermans-hitch|Lighterman's Hitch|lightermans-hitch-knot|Hitch|2|Boating|Tugboat hitch
=A sequence of turns used to secure a line around a bollard.
+Practice rope and a smooth vertical post.
!Real mooring loads, surges and bollard geometry need proper marine practice. Keep fingers out of loaded turns.
?Alternating bights cross around the standing part and settle neatly around the post.
xPiling loose turns on top of each other makes inspection and release difficult.
>Make the round turn::Pass the working rope around the bollard twice, keeping both initial turns at its base.
>Make a folded section::Take a bight in the working section, leaving the standing part leading away from the bollard.
>Pass under and over::Carry the bight under the standing part, then open it over the top of the bollard.
>Add a turn and repeat::Make an additional turn around the bollard, then place another bight under the standing part and over the bollard as shown.
>Continue and dress::Add further alternating turns as appropriate to the practice example. Snug every turn and keep hands clear of any loaded section.

@marlinspike-hitch|Marlinspike Hitch|marlinspike-hitch-knot|Hitch|2|Camping,Boating|Marlinespike hitch
=Insert a removable toggle into a temporary rope hitch.
+Soft practice rope and a smooth, blunt wooden toggle.
!A weak or poorly seated toggle can break or eject. This is not instruction for a hammock suspension or supporting a person.
?The bight comes from the standing part, so a light pull on that part closes the hitch around the toggle.
xInserting the toggle into the wrong opening can create a hitch that pulls straight out.
>Identify the standing part::Lay the rope on the table and mark the side that will be pulled. The bight must come from this side.
>Make a crossing loop::Form a small loop in the rope and keep its opening loose.
>Pull a standing-part bight through::Fold a section of the marked standing part and draw that fold through the crossing loop.
>Insert the toggle::Pass the smooth toggle through the emerging bight, centring it so both ends extend well beyond the rope.
>Seat and inspect::Apply only light hand tension to the standing part and verify that it tightens the grip. Unload completely before removing the toggle.

@midshipmans-hitch|Midshipman's Hitch|midshipmans-hitch-knot|Friction hitch|2|Camping,Boating|Awning hitch with finishing half hitch
=Adjust the tension of a light guyline using a gripping loop.
+Soft practice cord and a small fixed post.
!Not a life-support hitch. Modern slippery cords may not grip reliably; keep the loop angle narrow and check under changing tension.
?The second inner turn is tucked beside the first to form the gripping Awning Hitch; a half hitch finishes outside it.
xLoosely stacking the inner turns without the wedging tuck can make a different adjustable hitch.
>Return from the post::Take the working end around the post and bring it back beside the standing line.
>Make the first inner turn::Wrap the tail around the standing part inside the large loop.
>Wedge the second turn::Wrap again, tucking this turn beside the first and snugging it into the gripping Awning Hitch pattern.
>Finish outside::Move the tail beyond the gripping turns and add a half hitch around the standing part in the same direction.
>Adjust without load::Slide the dressed hitch when the line is slack, then lightly tension the standing line to check that it holds its position.

@square-lashing|Square Lashing|square-lashing-knot|Lashing|2|Camping|Square lashing knot
=Bind two touching poles together at a right angle.
+Two small smooth practice sticks and a long soft cord.
!Practice on a tabletop model. Real structures, platforms and ladders require competent design and inspection, not just a familiar knot.
?Wrapping turns bind both sticks; perpendicular frapping turns squeeze those wraps together between the sticks.
xContinuing around the poles instead of frapping between them misses the tightening stage.
>Anchor the cord::Cross the sticks at a right angle and tie a Clove Hitch around one near the crossing. Twist the short tail alongside the working part.
>Wrap the crossing::Lead the cord over and under the two sticks in a square path around their meeting point.
>Add close wraps::Make three or four circuits, adding turns inside the previous ones on one stick and outside them on the other.
>Frap between the sticks::Take three or four turns around the wrapping bundle between the sticks, pulling them snug to compress the wraps.
>Secure the finish::Finish with two or three tight half hitches as shown. Inspect the model from all sides before trying a light hand movement.

@monkeys-fist|Monkey's Fist|monkeys-fist-knot|Decorative|3|Decorative,Boating|Monkey fist
=A decorative rope ball built from three perpendicular groups of wraps.
+Soft cord only; use no core or a small, soft scrap-cord core.
!Never insert metal, stones or other hard weights. Keep it decorative and do not swing or throw it toward anyone.
?Three equal groups of wraps encircle one another at right angles, with no loose coil on the outside.
xTightening a single turn fully before moving around the knot traps uneven slack.
>Make the first group::Wrap the working end around two fingers three times, keeping the turns parallel.
>Turn ninety degrees::Wrap three more turns around the first group, in the perpendicular direction.
>Find the centre passage::Slide the developing ball from your fingers while keeping the two groups open enough to see the central hole.
>Make the third group::Pass three wraps through that centre, around the second group and inside the first, following the demonstrated route.
>Dress patiently::Starting at one end, move small amounts of slack along every turn in order. Repeat until the ball is firm but remains soft and unweighted.

@mooring-hitch|Mooring Hitch|mooring-hitch-knot|Quick release|2|Boating,Camping|
=A slipped attachment that can be released by its free tail.
+Practice cord and a smooth horizontal post.
!An accidental pull on the release tail can undo it. Not for climbing, a person, or an unattended critical load.
?The tail bight crosses the original loop and passes under the standing part before the knot is snugged.
xPutting the release bight into the wrong side of the initial loop changes the hitch.
>Pass behind and over::Take the rope behind the post, then bring it over the top to the front.
>Make a tail-side loop::Form a small loop so the free tail lies against the standing part.
>Fold the remaining tail::Create a bight in the loose tail, leaving enough free end to release it later.
>Lock across the loop::Carry that bight across the initial loop and underneath the standing part, following the demonstration.
>Set and rehearse::Draw the hitch snug with light hand tension. Remove tension and pull the release tail to check that it comes apart.

@nail-knot|Nail Knot|nail-knot|Fishing join|2|Fishing|
=Bind a thinner leader to a thicker line using a small tube as a guide.
+Two practice lines and a short, smooth drinking-straw segment.
!The line coating and materials affect the connection. Use a smooth tube rather than a sharp nail while learning.
?Six neat coils surround both lines; the tag runs back through their centre after the tube is removed.
xWithdrawing the tube before the tag is threaded loses the passage through the coils.
>Overlap the lines::Lay the thinner and thicker lines beside one another with their ends pointing in opposite directions.
>Add the guide tube::Hold a short tube alongside the overlapped section.
>Wind six turns::Wrap the thin tag around both lines and the tube six times, keeping the turns beside each other.
>Thread through the tube::Feed the tag through the tube so it will run back underneath all the coils. Gently snug the coils before sliding the tube out.
>Set the connection::Moisten as appropriate and draw the turns together without overlap. Test before trimming the free ends.

@non-slip-mono|Non-Slip Mono Loop|non-slip-mono-knot|Fishing loop|2|Fishing|Kreh loop
=Give a lure room to move on a small fixed loop.
+Practice monofilament and a blunt lure-eye substitute.
!The correct number of wraps depends on line diameter. Check the loop size before setting and test the finished connection.
?The tag returns from the wrap stack through the overhand opening toward the lure, and the finished loop remains fixed.
xTightening the initial overhand knot before completing the wraps blocks the finishing tuck.
>Make a loose overhand::Tie an open overhand knot near the line end, leaving a generous tag.
>Go through the eye and back::Pass the tag through the lure eye and back through the loose overhand opening toward the standing line.
>Make the wrap stack::Wrap the tag around the standing line about five times for the illustrated method. Actual line diameter may require a different count.
>Return toward the lure::Bring the tag back from the wrap stack through the overhand opening toward the eye, following the displayed crossing.
>Set the eye size::Adjust the lure loop while everything is loose. Moisten, dress and tighten the knot, then inspect and test before trimming.

@orvis|Orvis Knot|orvis-knot|Fishing attachment|2|Fishing|
=A small eye attachment with a doubled final tuck.
+Practice fishing line and a blunt ring.
!Inspect carefully before use; a compact shape alone does not demonstrate correct threading or adequate strength.
?The final opening contains two tag passes, and the eye-side crossing stays intact as the knot sets.
xPutting the extra pass through the first opening instead of the second changes the knot.
>Thread the eye::Pass the tag through the eye and bring it across the standing line.
>Circle and return::Take the tag around the standing line and back through the first, eye-side loop.
>Use the new opening::Bring the tag back through the second loop formed by that movement.
>Add a second pass::Pass the tag through this same second opening once more, keeping both small turns orderly.
>Set carefully::Moisten as appropriate and ease the knot to the eye, then tighten and inspect before trimming the tag.

@overhand|Overhand Knot|overhand-knot|Stopper|1|Everyday,Camping|Thumb knot
=The simplest stopper and a building block for many other knots.
+One soft practice rope.
!Small and prone to jamming. A stopper must be larger than the opening it is intended to block; this is not a rope join.
?The rope crosses once and the working end passes through the resulting loop once.
xA bight through the opening makes a slipped version instead of the plain stopper.
>Find the working end::Choose a short end to move and leave the rest of the rope as the standing part.
>Make a crossing::Curve the working end across the standing part to create a small loop.
>Put the end through::Pass the tip through that loop once, taking the whole tail through rather than a folded bight.
>Take out the slack::Hold the standing part and tail, then draw them gently apart.
>Inspect the stopper::Leave a visible tail and examine the compact crossing. Untie it before pulling it so tight that it jams.

@palomar|Palomar Knot|palomar-knot|Fishing attachment|1|Fishing|
=Attach an eye using a doubled line and a large finishing loop.
+Practice line and a blunt hook substitute small enough to pass through the loop.
!Keep the doubled strands uncrossed. Line and lure manufacturers may prescribe different attachments or setting methods.
?The entire hook or lure passes through the doubled loop, which settles around the knot rather than catching on the eye.
xLeaving the finishing loop around only the hook's point or eye prevents correct seating.
>Double the line::Fold enough line to pass a bight through the eye, leaving a substantial loop and tag.
>Pass through the eye::Feed the doubled bight through the eye without twisting its two strands.
>Tie a loose overhand::Use the doubled bight to make an overhand knot around the doubled standing section. Keep it open.
>Take the loop over the object::Pass the entire hook or lure through the large bight and bring the bight down around the forming knot.
>Seat the knot::Moisten, then draw the standing and tag ends together so both strands settle evenly. Test before trimming.

@perfection-loop|Perfection Loop|perfection-loop-knot|Fishing loop|2|Fishing|Angler's loop
=A small, tidy fixed loop at the end of a leader.
+Practice monofilament or a soft fine cord.
!Can jam and may not suit every line material. Inspect the tag's position before the final pull.
?The second loop has been pulled through the first, with the tag captured between the original loops.
xPulling the first loop through the second reverses the intended structure.
>Make the first loop::Form a small loop near the line end, with the tag crossing behind the standing line.
>Lay a second loop on top::Circle the tag again to create another loop resting above the first.
>Place the tag between::Bring the remaining tag across the gap between the two loops and hold it there.
>Pull the top loop through::Reach through the lower loop, grasp the upper loop and draw it through to form the finished eye.
>Dress and set::Pull the standing line while holding the new eye. Moisten actual line, set the knot and inspect the captured tag before trimming.

@pile-hitch|Pile Hitch|pile-hitch-knot|Hitch|1|Boating,Everyday|
=Attach a doubled rope to a post without threading its end.
+Practice cord and a post with an accessible top.
!Requires access over the post top and suitable post geometry. Not a substitute for a properly designed mooring system.
?The doubled rope crosses around the standing sections and the whole bight goes over the post.
xDropping a plain bight over the post without the lower crossing does not make the hitch.
>Make a bight::Fold a generous middle section of rope, keeping its two legs side by side.
>Pass around the post::Carry the bight around the lower part of the post.
>Cross beneath the standing parts::Bring the doubled tip underneath the rope sections that lead away from the post.
>Lift over the top::Open the bight and pass the entire fold over the top of the post.
>Seat the crossing::Pull the standing sections to draw the hitch downward and snugly around the post. Keep the crossing visible for inspection.

@poachers-knot|Poacher's Knot|poachers-knot|Sliding loop|2|Everyday,Camping|Double-overhand sliding loop
=A sliding eye formed by a double-overhand knot around the standing part.
+Soft practice rope and a small inanimate object.
!This loop tightens under pull. Never place it around a person, limb or animal; it can jam hard.
?Two wraps form a barrel around the standing part, which slides through the barrel to change the eye size.
xMistaking this sliding eye for a fixed loop can crush or constrict the object inside it.

>Wrap both sections::Carry the tail around the standing part and its own returning section once.
>Add another wrap::Make a second adjacent turn around those same sections.
>Thread back through::Pass the tail backward through the centre of both turns to form a double-overhand barrel.
>Dress and slide::Snug the barrel on the standing part. Use a light pull to observe the eye closing, then release it without a real load.

@pratt|Pratt Necktie Knot|tie-a-pratt-necktie-knot|Necktie|2|Neckties|Shelby knot
=A balanced tie knot that starts with the seam facing outward.
+A fabric necktie and mirror.
!Keep the collar comfortable. Use fabric neckwear, never practice cord, around your neck.
?The tie starts inside out, but its wide blade faces outward once it passes through the front band.
xStarting with the tie's face outward changes the orientation of the finished blade.
>Start seam outward::Drape the tie inside out, with the wide end longer. Cross the wide end underneath the narrow end.
>Make the first turn::Bring the wide end up, over and down through the neck opening, drawing it to the side.
>Make the front band::Carry the wide end horizontally across the front of the narrow end and forming knot.
>Come up from behind::Pass the wide end upward through the neck opening, keeping the front band loose.
>Feed through and shape::Bring the wide blade down through the front band. Gently tighten and centre the knot without constricting the collar.

@prusik|Prusik Knot|prusik-knot|Friction hitch|2|Specialist|Prusik hitch

+A closed practice cord loop and thicker host rope, with no suspended load.
!Climbing use requires compatible cord, the right wrap count, backups and qualified instruction. Do not test this by hanging from it.
?The wraps are parallel and balanced on both sides; the loop's joining knot stays outside the gripping turns.
xCrossed wraps or an unsuitable cord-to-rope diameter ratio can prevent reliable gripping.

@rolling-hitch|Rolling Hitch|rolling-hitch-knot|Friction hitch|2|Boating,Camping|
=A directional attachment for pulling along another rope.
+Two contrasting practice ropes with the host rope held straight.
!The version shown is the rope-gripping pattern. Modern slick rope, reversed pull or a pole application may need a different knot.
?The extra gripping turn is tucked between the first turn and the standing part, with the final half hitch beyond it.
xLaying the second turn loosely beside the first, without the wedging tuck, changes the grip.
>Choose the pull direction::Lay the working line beside the host rope and identify the direction it will pull along it.
>Make the first turn::Wrap the tail around the host rope to make a loose half hitch.
>Wedge the next turn::Continue around over the first turn, then tuck this new turn between that first turn and the standing part.
>Finish beyond the grip::Add a half hitch around the host rope beyond the wedged turns, following the same rotational direction.
>Dress and observe::Snug the hitch neatly; the host rope may show a small bend. Test only with a light pull in the intended direction.
