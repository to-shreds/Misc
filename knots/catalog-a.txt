@double-alpine-butterfly-loop|Double Alpine Butterfly Loop|double-alpine-butterfly-loop-knot|Fixed loop|3|Camping,Specialist|Double butterfly loop
=Make two fixed eyes in the middle of a rope.
+About two metres of soft practice rope; keep hand wraps loose.
!Two eyes are not automatically an equalized anchor. Never use this practice arrangement to suspend a person.
?Two neighbouring turns become the eyes, while the two outside turns form the knot's shoulders.
xPicking up an outside turn instead of the middle pair gives a different structure.
>Lay out four turns::Wrap the rope so four parallel sections cross your palm, keeping everything loose enough to remove easily.
>Identify the eye sections::Position the second and third turns toward your fingertips, with the first and fourth nearer your thumb.
>Lift the middle pair::Pick up the two fingertip-side turns together and draw them out slightly.
>Pass under the outside pair::Fold those two turns around and up beneath the other two sections, following the demonstration. These folds become the two eyes.
>Slide off and dress::Remove your hand before tightening. Adjust the eye sizes, then draw the standing parts apart while keeping all turns orderly.

@albright|Albright Knot|albright-knot|Fishing join|2|Fishing|
=Join a thin fishing line to a substantially thicker leader.
+Two contrasting practice lines; use cord first to learn the path.
!Line materials and diameters affect the required turns and performance. Follow line-maker guidance and test the connection before fishing.
?The thin line enters and leaves the thick-line loop on the same side; its coils are orderly.
xExiting on the opposite side can make the wraps unravel rather than lock.
>Fold the thicker line::Make a narrow U-shaped bight in the thicker leader. Hold both legs together.
>Thread the thin line::Feed a generous tag end of the thinner line through the bight.
>Wrap toward the fold::Turn the tag back and make about ten close wraps around both thick-line legs and its own standing section.
>Exit beside the entry::Put the tag through the bight again so both thin-line ends emerge on the same side.
>Set carefully::Wet as appropriate for the line, ease the coils together, and pull the standing lines firmly. Check the joint before trimming.

@alpine-butterfly-bend|Alpine Butterfly Bend|alpine-butterfly-bend-knot|Bend|2|Camping,Specialist|Butterfly bend
=An interlocking join for two ropes of similar diameter.
+Two similar soft cords in different colours.
!This practice description is not a climbing-rope joining procedure. Use material-specific qualified guidance for critical applications.
?Both standing parts leave on opposite sides, while the two independent tails emerge from the butterfly centre.
xA superficially similar pair of loops is not enough; reversing one crossing changes the knot.
>Join the tips temporarily::Hold the two rope tips together with a small piece of removable tape. This is only a tying aid, not part of the finished bend.
>Wrap around your hand::Wind the joined ropes around your open hand, placing the temporary join near your fingertips. Continue around for another loose turn.
>Lift the joined section::Fold the joined section back over the other rope sections, keeping the temporary join together.
>Tuck beneath the other sections::Bring the joined section up underneath the other wraps, following the butterfly path shown in the demonstration.
>Remove and inspect::Slide the knot off your hand, dress it gently and remove the temporary tape. Confirm two separate, generous tails remain before setting.

@alpine-butterfly-loop|Alpine Butterfly Loop|alpine-butterfly-loop-knot|Fixed loop|2|Camping,Specialist|Lineman's loop,Butterfly loop
=Make a fixed loop in the middle of a rope without using its ends.
+About 1.5 metres of soft practice rope.
!Do not treat this as approval for climbing or isolating a damaged rope. Critical rope systems need qualified instruction.
?The loop exits between two balanced shoulders. Both standing parts run away from opposite sides.
xTaking the wrong strand off your hand can form a different, unstable knot.
>Arrange three strands::Wrap the rope around your open hand twice so that three parallel sections lie across your palm. Put the section that will become the loop nearest your fingertips.
>Lift the loop section::Pick up the fingertip-side section and carry it over the other two sections.
>Go around both::Continue that lifted bight behind and around the other two strands, not just one.
>Return through the middle::Bring the bight up through the opening between those two strands and pull it clear of your hand.
>Shape the butterfly::Hold the loop while pulling the two standing parts apart. Ease out crossed strands and snug the knot evenly.

@anchor-hitch|Anchor Hitch|anchor-hitch-knot|Hitch|2|Boating|Fisherman's bend,Anchor bend
=Attach a line to a ring with two turns and a tucked finishing hitch.
+Practice cord and a smooth ring or post.
!For a real anchor, chafe protection, rope selection and securing the tail require proper marine practice. Do not trust an unattended practice knot.
?The first locking tuck runs under the slack second turn around the ring; a further half hitch surrounds the standing part.
xTying two ordinary half hitches after the turns produces a different hitch.
>Make two turns::Pass the working end through the ring twice. Leave the second turn slack enough for the tail to pass underneath it.
>Cross the standing part::Bring the tail over the standing part to begin the locking hitch.
>Tuck under the slack turn::Feed the tail under the original loose second turn around the ring, then draw this tuck snug.
>Add the finishing half hitch::Take the tail around the standing part again and through its own opening in the same rotational direction.
>Dress the attachment::Snug the turns against the ring, check the locking tuck and leave a generous visible tail for practice.

@arbor|Arbor Knot|arbor-knot|Fishing attachment|1|Fishing|Spool knot
=Fasten fishing line around the centre of a reel spool.
+Practice line and an empty spool or smooth cylinder.
!This secures the spool connection, not a fish-bearing terminal connection. Slippery braid may need the reel manufacturer's prescribed backing.
?An overhand stopper on the tag prevents it pulling through the sliding overhand knot.
xOmitting the tiny stopper lets the tag pull through the main knot.
>Go around the spool::Take the line around the spool's arbor and bring the tag back alongside the standing line.
>Tie around the line::With the tag, tie a loose overhand knot around the standing line. Do not tie both ends together as a square knot.
>Add the stopper::Tie a separate small overhand knot near the tip of the tag.
>Slide into place::Pull the standing line to draw the first knot against the arbor. The tag stopper should seat against it.
>Check the spool::Pull gently to check the line stays attached and that the reel can turn freely before winding on more line.

@ashley-bend|Ashley Bend|ashley-bend-knot|Bend|2|Camping,Boating|
=Join similar ropes using interlocked overhand structures.
+Two similar cords in contrasting colours.
!Material, loading and dressing matter. Do not substitute this practice join into a life-support system.
?As the knot sets, each tail rotates toward the opposite rope's standing part. Trace the central crossings, not just the outline.
xCrossing the tails incorrectly in the centre creates a different bend.
>Make two open folds::Form a U-shaped bight near each rope end, keeping the tails long.
>Interlock the bights::Place the two folds through one another, with their curved ends overlapping.
>Cross the first tail::Take one tail over its own standing part, under both ropes, and up through the centre beside its own standing section.
>Repeat with the other tail::Follow the matching route with the other colour: over itself, under the pair, then up through the centre.
>Dress and allow rotation::Take up slack slowly. Each tail turns toward the other rope's standing part as the knot forms; check this arrangement before tightening.

@ashley-stopper|Ashley Stopper Knot|ashley-stopper-knot|Stopper|2|Everyday,Boating|Oysterman's stopper
=A bulky, three-sided stopper for a rope end.
+One soft rope with a long working end.
!A stopper must be larger than the opening it is meant to block. It is not an anchor or a substitute for a rated termination.
?Three lobes bear against the opening, with the tail captured through the final loop.
xPulling the wrong part too early collapses the slip-loop needed for the final tuck.
>Form a small slip-loop::Make a crossing loop, then draw a bight of the standing part through it rather than feeding the tail all the way through.
>Keep the bight open::Hold the emerging bight so it cannot disappear while you make the next tuck.
>Pass the tail through::Take the working end through the open bight. Leave a visible length projecting beyond it.
>Close the collar::Pull the standing part to shrink that bight around the tail.
>Seat the stopper::Work the three lobes into a compact shape, then pull each part carefully to remove remaining slack.

@bimini-twist|Bimini Twist|bimini-twist-knot|Fishing loop|3|Fishing|
=Make a doubled-line section with a long, wrapped twist.
+Long practice line, a smooth support for the loop, and eye protection when tensioning real line.
!Wrap count and finish vary with line material. Learn with slack cord; line under high tension can cut or recoil.
?A uniform outer wrap covers the inner twists; the finishing hitches lock the doubled line without bunching.
xLetting the tension vanish while the tag rolls down leaves gaps and loose coils.
>Make a long doubled loop::Double a generous length of line, keeping a long tag for the finishing work.
>Twist the legs::Rotate the loop to make at least twenty full twists for this practice method. Keep the doubled section under light, even tension.
>Spread the loop::Widen the loop legs while controlling the tag. The tag should begin wrapping back down over the twists.
>Cover the twists::Guide the returning coils closely together until they cover the twisted section.
>Lock and finish::Secure the end of the wrap with a half hitch, then follow the demonstrated multi-turn finishing hitch around the doubled line. Set and inspect before trimming.

@blakes-hitch|Blake's Hitch|blakes-hitch-knot|Friction hitch|3|Specialist|
=A directional friction hitch studied in arborist rope work.
+Two compatible soft practice ropes; no suspended person or load.
!Never climb on a hitch learned from this page alone. Rope compatibility, backups, heat and professional instruction are essential.
?Four neat wraps surround the host rope; the tail emerges between the second and third wraps.
xPutting the tail under all four wraps changes the gripping behaviour.
>Make four turns::Wrap the working rope around the host rope four times, progressing upward. Leave a long tail.
>Bring the tail down::Carry the working end back alongside the outside of the turns toward their lower end.
>Go around your own line::Pass it around the working rope's standing section, then behind the host rope.
>Tuck under two wraps::Feed the tail upward beneath the bottom two turns only, so it exits halfway up the stack.
>Dress for inspection::Align and snug all turns. Leave the required finishing details to the rope-system procedure you are learning under qualified supervision.

@blood-knot|Blood Knot|blood-knot|Fishing join|2|Fishing|Barrel knot
=A compact join between fishing lines of similar diameter.
+Two contrasting fishing lines or fine practice cords.
!Best suited to compatible, similar-diameter lines. Turn counts should be adjusted to the actual line maker's guidance.
?The two tags emerge from opposite sides of the central opening; the coils form two orderly barrels.
xBoth tags leaving on the same side is the classic threading error.
>Overlap the lines::Lay the ends alongside one another in opposite directions, with enough overlap for several wraps.
>Wrap the first tag::Turn one tag around the other line about five times. Bring it back and tuck it through the gap at the original crossing.
>Wrap the second tag::Repeat with the other tag around the first line, building a matching set of coils.
>Opposite exit::Feed the second tag through the same central gap, but from the opposite side to the first tag.
>Draw together::Moisten as appropriate and slowly pull the standing lines apart. Help the coils settle neatly before the final pull and trim.

@boom-hitch|Boom Hitch|boom-hitch-knot|Hitch|3|Boating|
=Fasten a line around a spar with a compact crossing pattern.
+Practice cord and a smooth dowel.
!Study only with light hand loads. The direction of pull and correct crossing order matter.
?Four correctly ordered turns surround the spar, the fourth tucks beneath the second, and the tail has an overhand stopper.
xCrossing a wrap over its neighbour at the wrong point can create a superficially similar but different hitch.
>Wrap toward one side::Take the first turn around the spar, leading it to one side of the standing part.
>Wrap toward the other side::Make the second turn around the spar toward the opposite side.
>Cover the first turn::Bring the third turn back toward the original side, laying it over the first turn.
>Tuck the fourth turn::Take the fourth turn outside the second turn and then beneath that second turn, preserving the crossing order.
>Add a stopper and dress::Tie an overhand stopper in the tail. Work slack out gradually without rearranging the turns, then inspect the front and back.

@bow-tie|Bow Tie|tie-a-bow-tie|Necktie|2|Neckties|
=Tie a real bow tie, with two folded wings rather than a pre-tied clip.
+A fabric bow tie; practice loosely away from your neck first.
!Keep it comfortable. Never practice neckwear tying with rope or tighten anything against breathing or circulation.
?Each side has one folded wing and one single fabric end, with the bow lying horizontally.
xTrying to feed a straight end through the rear opening instead of a folded bight prevents the bow forming.
>Offset the ends::Drape the tie with one end a little longer. Cross the longer end over the shorter one.
>Make the first tie::Bring the longer end up through the neck opening and rest it out of the way.
>Fold the front wing::Fold the shorter end horizontally at its widest part to make the front half of the bow.
>Drape and fold::Bring the longer end down over the middle of that bow. Fold its wide part into a second bow-shaped bight.
>Thread the rear wing::Push the folded bight through the opening behind the front bow. Adjust by gently pulling opposing folded wings and straight ends.

@bowline|Bowline|bowline-knot|Fixed loop|2|Everyday,Camping,Boating|Bowline knot
=Make a fixed-size eye at the end of a rope.
+About one metre of soft rope; a post is optional.
!A bowline can loosen when slack or repeatedly shaken. Do not use this basic form for climbing or personal support without qualified instruction.
?The tail lies inside the big loop and the small collar wraps around the standing part. The loop does not slide closed.
xPassing around a leg of the big loop instead of the standing part creates the wrong structure.
>Make the small crossing::Form a small overhand loop in the standing part. Leave plenty of working end for the large finished loop.
>Bring the end through::Curve the working end around to form the large eye, then bring its tip up through the small loop.
>Go around the standing part::Take the tip behind the long standing part, not around either leg of the large eye.
>Return through the opening::Feed the tip back down through the original small loop, beside its first pass.
>Dress the collar::Hold the tail and the adjacent leg together while pulling the standing part. Adjust the large eye before fully snugging.

@bowline-on-a-bight|Bowline on a Bight|bowline-on-a-bight-knot|Fixed loop|2|Boating,Specialist|Double-loop bowline
=Create two loops using a doubled section of rope.
+About 1.5 metres of practice rope.
!Two loops do not make this an approved harness or rescue seat. Never experiment on a person.
?The final collar encircles both standing sections and the two large loops sit alongside each other.
xFeeding the bight back through as if tying a normal bowline skips the essential folding step.
>Double the rope::Take a long bight and treat its two strands as one. Make a small crossing loop in the doubled section.
>Pass the bight through::Push the folded tip of the bight through that crossing loop. Keep the emerging loop fairly small.
>Open the tip::Spread this emerging bight and fold it down over the two large loops below it.
>Move it behind::Continue taking the spread bight behind the whole knot, up around the two standing sections.
>Balance and tighten::Adjust the two large eyes to the desired size, then snug the collar and both standing parts evenly.

@buntline-hitch|Buntline Hitch|buntline-hitch-knot|Hitch|2|Boating,Everyday|
=A compact hitch for fastening a rope end to a ring or small object.
+Practice rope and a ring or smooth post.
!Can jam hard after loading. Not a quick-release hitch and not a universal answer for slippery modern rope.
?The two half-hitch turns form a clove hitch around the standing part, with the final turn on the object side.
xFinishing away from the object produces the different two-half-hitches arrangement.
>Go around the object::Pass the tail around the post or through the ring and return beside the standing part.
>Circle the standing part::Make a complete turn around the standing section, leaving the wrapping loose. Do not yet tighten it as a separate half hitch.
>Return beside the object::Lead the tail through the opening next to the post, following the inverted clove-hitch path in the demonstration.
>Complete the half hitch::Take the tail around and tuck it beneath the final crossing to finish the small clove-hitch structure around the standing part.
>Slide and set::Draw the hitch against the object and snug all turns. Keep a generous tail and remember this hitch can jam tightly.

@butchers-knot|Butcher's Knot|butchers-knot|Binding|2|Everyday|Butchers knot
=A sliding binding knot for tying a compact bundle.
+Food-safe cotton twine for food; soft cord and a towel for practice.
!Use only food-safe materials for cooking. Never use synthetic practice cord in an oven or tie around a limb.
?A sliding overhand grips around the bundle; a loop from the long section is tightened over the short end to secure it.
xTrimming before securing the finishing hitch lets the binding loosen.
>Go around the bundle::Wrap the twine around the practice bundle, leaving a short end and a longer working section.
>Tie around the long section::Use the short end to make one overhand knot around the long standing section. Keep its orientation matched to the demonstration.
>Draw the binding snug::Pull the long section through the overhand knot to remove slack around the bundle without crushing it.
>Place the locking loop::Make a small loop in the long section around your fingers, then slide that loop over the short end.
>Set the finish::Draw both ends to snug the locking turn against the initial knot. Check the binding before carefully trimming excess twine.

@carrick-bend|Carrick Bend|carrick-bend-knot|Bend|3|Boating|Full carrick bend
=Interweave two rope ends in a balanced, easily inspected pattern.
+Two similar ropes in contrasting colours, laid on a flat surface.
!The decorative flat layout changes when loaded. Critical applications require correct dressing and suitable equipment.
?In the flat layout each rope alternates over and under; tails exit diagonally opposite. Trace rather than guess.
xMissing the central underpass creates an insecure look-alike.
>Lay the first loop::Make an open loop in the first rope with its tail underneath its standing part. Keep this flat throughout the weaving stage.
>Place the second rope::Bring the second tail underneath the first loop, leaving enough length to weave back across it.
>Go around the two ends::Carry the second tail over the first standing part and under the first tail, retaining the flat layout.
>Weave across the loop::Take the second tail over the first loop's edge, under its own second-rope section at the centre, then over the opposite edge.
>Trace and dress::Check the alternating over-under crossings before pulling the standing parts. Let the flat pattern settle naturally, maintaining long tails.

@chain-sinnet|Chain Sinnet|chain-sinnet-knot|Decorative|1|Everyday,Decorative|Daisy chain,Monkey braid
=Shorten an idle cord into an easy-to-release chain.
+One long soft cord.
!For storage or decoration, not a load-bearing chain or a way to repair damaged rope.
?Each link is a bight pulled through the previous bight; the last link is secured with the actual end.
xFailing to pass the end through the last loop leaves a chain that can unravel immediately.
>Start a slip-loop::Make a loose overhand loop and pull a bight of the long working section through it.
>Make another bight::Fold the working section again a little farther along the rope.
>Build one link::Pull the new fold through the first loop and snug the old loop around it, leaving the new loop open.
>Repeat the rhythm::Keep drawing a fresh bight through the current loop until the rope has shortened enough.
>Lock or release::For storage, pass the actual free end through the final loop. To unravel, remove that end and pull the chain free.

@cleat-hitch|Cleat Hitch|cleat-hitch-knot-dock-line|Hitch|1|Boating|Dock-line cleat hitch
=Make orderly turns around a cleat to secure a dock line.
+Practice line and a model cleat with two horns.
!Boat size, cleat geometry and load matter. Keep hands clear of loaded turns; this is not mooring-system training.
?The line approaches the far horn first, crosses neatly, and finishes with a locking hitch whose lines lie parallel.
xA reversed final loop crosses over itself and can work loose.
>Approach the far horn::Bring the line around the base on the side farthest from the direction of pull.
>Complete the base turn::Continue around the base of the cleat, keeping the initial turn below the horns.
>Cross the top::Lead the working end diagonally over the centre and around the opposite horn.
>Cross back::Return diagonally across the centre, making a clean figure-eight pattern around the horns.
>Lock the final turn::Make a loop, turn it so its tail lies beneath the crossing, and drop it over the final horn. Snug the parallel strands.

@clove-hitch|Clove Hitch|clove-hitch-knot-rope-end|Hitch|1|Everyday,Camping,Boating|
=A fast temporary attachment around a post or pole.
+Soft rope and a smooth practice post.
!Can slip or jam, especially under changing direction or intermittent loads. Do not rely on it alone for a critical or unattended load.
?Two turns encircle the post, crossed by one diagonal bar; the ends leave on opposite sides.
xTwo turns without tucking beneath the diagonal do not complete the hitch.
>Make the first turn::Take the working end completely around the post.
>Cross the standing part::Bring the tail diagonally over the standing part on the front of the post.
>Go around again::Continue behind the post to make a second full turn alongside the first.
>Tuck beneath the diagonal::Slide the working end under the crossing bar on the front, not through an unrelated side loop.
>Dress and inspect::Pull the two ends gently in opposite directions. Keep the turns adjacent and the diagonal crossing visible.

@cobra-lanyard|Cobra Lanyard|cobra-lanyard-knot|Decorative|2|Decorative|Solomon bar,Square-knot sinnet
=A flat decorative band made from alternating half knots.
+Two working cord ends arranged around two central filler strands.
!Decoration only. Do not make non-breakaway neckwear or assume a bracelet is safety equipment.
?Alternating left and right starts produce a flat band. A repeated same-side start makes a spiral instead.
xPulling the filler strands into the working loops distorts the band.
>Set the centre::Hold two filler strands straight and parallel. Place one working end on each side.
>Cross from the left::Take the left working end across the front of both fillers, making a side loop on the left.
>Return from the right::Bring the right end over that left tail, behind the fillers, and out through the left-side loop. Snug.
>Reverse the direction::Now begin from the right, crossing in front. Send the left end over, behind, and through the right-side loop.
>Build the band::Alternate these two half knots, keeping equal tension and the fillers straight. Finish the ends appropriately for your craft project.

@constrictor|Constrictor Knot|constrictor-knot-rope-end-method|Binding|2|Everyday,Camping|Constrictor hitch
=A very tight binding for a sack neck or temporary whipping.
+Soft cord and a rounded bundle or dowel.
!May become impossible to untie and require cutting. Never tie around a person or an animal.
?An overhand crossing is trapped beneath a diagonal riding turn on the curved surface.
xA plain clove hitch lacks the extra trapped overhand crossing.
>Wrap and cross::Take the working end around the bundle, then cross it diagonally over the standing part.
>Make the second turn::Go around the bundle again, laying the new turn beside the first.
>Pass over the standing part::Bring the working end across the standing section at the front.
>Tuck under the riding turn::Feed the tail beneath the diagonal crossing, trapping the overhand-shaped crossing under it.
>Set the binding::Pull both ends firmly and evenly. Inspect before tightening fully because the knot can bite down permanently.

@cow-hitch|Cow Hitch|cow-hitch-knot-end-method|Hitch|1|Everyday,Decorative|Lark's head
=A simple symmetrical hitch around a bar or ring.
+Practice cord and a dowel.
!Security depends on how the two ends are loaded. A single loaded end can slip; this is not a universal animal tie.
?The two turns face opposite directions, with their connecting section crossing over the ends.
xMaking both turns in the same direction forms a different hitch.
>Make the first turn::Take the working end around the bar and bring it to the front.
>Cross the standing part::Carry the working end across the standing part, preparing to reverse direction.
>Turn back the other way::Pass around the bar again, this time in the opposite direction to your first turn.
>Feed through the centre::Tuck the working end under the connecting section between the two turns.
>Set both ends::Draw both ends together to seat the paired turns. Inspect how it behaves when only one end is pulled lightly.

@crown-knot|Crown Knot|crown-knot|Decorative|2|Decorative|Three-strand crown
=Turn three loose strands inward to begin a back splice or decorative finish.
+Three soft strands held together at their base; no live electrical cable.
!A crown alone is not a complete back splice or dependable rope termination.
?Each strand crosses over its neighbour and through the next opening, creating a three-part inward crown.
xTucking the final strand outside the original loop breaks the cyclic pattern.
>Separate three strands::Hold the three strand bases together with their tips spread out evenly.
>Fold the first inward::Lay the first strand across the centre and over the second strand, leaving a small open bend.
>Follow with the second::Bring the second strand over the first strand's end and across the third strand.
>Close the cycle::Take the third strand over the second end and through the bend left by the first strand.
>Draw evenly::Tighten each end a little at a time until a compact crown sits on the strand bases.
