BEFORE THE FIRST LOOP

Good knots begin
with good habits.

You do not need a workshop. Start with about a metre of soft, easy-to-see cord and something sturdy to practice around. A second colour makes rope-joining knots much easier to follow.

Practice guide, not a qualification.

Do not use this guide alone for climbing, rescue, lifting people, securing a vehicle load, animal restraint, or any safety-critical rigging. Get qualified instruction, use rated compatible equipment, and follow its manufacturer’s procedures. A correctly shaped knot can still fail in the wrong rope or application.

Speak rope.

Terminology illustration only. A bight is a fold, not necessarily the final shape of a knot.

Working end / tag end

The tip you move through the knot. “Tag end” is common in fishing. The spare end left after tying is the tail.

Standing part

The longer section leading away from the knot, often toward the load. Keep track of it when the instructions say “around the standing part.”

Bight / loop / turn

A bight is an uncrossed U-shaped fold. In this guide a loop usually means a crossing or closed curve. A turn goes around an object; a round turn travels farther around it before coming back.

Dress / set / check

Dress: arrange the strands neatly. Set: snug the knot by pulling the appropriate parts. Check: trace the entire rope path, including the exits and tails.

A knot for the job.

Stopper

Makes a rope end bulkier so it does not pull through a small opening. Begin with the overhand or figure-eight stopper.

Loop

Makes an eye in the rope. A fixed loop stays the same size; a sliding loop does not. Never substitute one for the other.

Bend

Joins separate ropes or lines. Material and diameter matter. A square knot is a binding knot, not a reliable bend for joining loaded ropes.

Hitch / binding

A hitch attaches to something. A binding knot closes around a bundle. Many rely on the object, friction, and a particular direction of pull.

Why do the pictures and videos need an internet connection?

Knotbook’s words and interface are local. The real tying demonstrations and their preview pictures are delivered by YouTube and credited to Animated Knots by Grog. They are not copied or re-hosted here. Pressing play loads an external player. The original illustrated source is always linked below it.

What if the player is blocked or does not load?

Use “Watch on YouTube” or “Illustrated source” beneath the demonstration. Schools, content blockers, offline browsers, or the video owner may restrict embedded playback. The written instructions remain available. Never guess a crossing you cannot see.

Are left-handed and right-handed versions different knots?

Many ordinary knots have equivalent mirror-image versions, but that is not a universal rule. Follow one complete method consistently. This guide deliberately does not mirror specialist diagrams or rewrite left/right instructions automatically.

How much tail should I leave?

There is no universal safe tail length for every rope, knot, or load. Practice with generous tails. For actual use, follow the equipment manufacturer and qualified instruction. Fishing knots have line-specific trimming and setting requirements.
